      How the rating works

      Every hour gets five scores from 0 to 1: size (how close the waves are to your board's sweet spot), wind (light offshore is perfect, onshore blows it out), swell power (longer period means cleaner, more powerful waves, and short chop mixed in counts against it), tide (; each break also likes the tide either rising or falling), and direction (how open the break is to the swell). They are multiplied together, with size counting most, then scaled to 10 on a curve. A 10 means every factor is perfect at once, which is rare. A normal fun day lands around 5 to 7.

      Wave size comes from the CDIP nearshore wave model run by Scripps Institution of Oceanography. It predicts the waves in 10 m of water straight off each break, already counting how the islands, La Jolla and Point Loma block and bend each swell. waive splits that into groundswell (12 s and longer), windswell (7 to 12 s) and chop, works out how big each swell breaks with a standard formula (longer swells break bigger), and adds them up. For the first day the forecast is pulled toward what the buoys are measuring right now. If the nearshore model is unavailable, waive falls back to two open-ocean wave models blended together.

      Wind uses the measured wind on Scripps Pier for the current hour and the forecast after that. Gusts count, onshore wind that has blown for three hours or more counts extra because the chop has built up, and chop lingers for a couple of hours after the wind drops.

      Low confidence shows when the two open-ocean wave models disagree by more than about a third, or when the nearshore forecast is far from what is being measured. Self-correction: every day waive saves its forecast, later compares it with the buoy, and adjusts the open-ocean forecast by the average miss.

      On the scale, Fair is about 5 to 7, Good is 7 and up, and Epic is 8.5 and up.

      Wetsuit suggestion. The chip next to the water temperature comes only from the Torrey Pines buoy's measured water temperature, never an estimate. If the buoy has no reading it says so. In Fine-tune you can say whether you run cold or warm, and the suggestion moves one step thicker or thinner. It is a general guide, not advice for your body.
